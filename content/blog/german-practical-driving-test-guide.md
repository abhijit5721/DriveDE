The short version: the practical exam for Class B (car) in Germany takes **55 minutes** total (about 25 minutes of that is actual driving), covers a **vehicle check, a drive in traffic, and three set maneuvers**, and roughly **one in three** candidates fail it. Know the structure and drill the mistakes that actually fail people, and you walk in with a real advantage.

## How the exam runs

The Class B test takes **55 minutes** overall, with around 25 minutes of pure driving time in traffic. In the car with you: your instructor (front passenger seat) and the examiner from TÜV or DEKRA (back seat).

1. **Vehicle check / technical questions.** The examiner asks 2 to 3 questions about the car, checking tire tread, explaining the lights, checking oil level. The questions come from an official published list and are entirely learnable. The [DriveDE app](https://www.drivede.app) has a free trainer for exactly this, covering every warning light.
2. **Drive in traffic.** Usually city streets, often a rural road or highway on-ramp too, depending on the test center. The examiner gives directions using standardized phrases.
3. **Set maneuvers.** Usually three, for example reverse parking, an emergency stop, and a three-point turn, worked into the drive.
4. **Result on the spot.** Pass means you get your license confirmation directly from the examiner. Fail means a short explanation, and the examiner names the decisive mistakes.

## The most common reasons people fail

The failure statistics have stayed stable for years, it's almost always the same categories:

1. **Right of way**, especially the *rechts vor links* (right-before-left) rule in residential areas with no signs. A single right-of-way mistake ends the test.
2. **Missing observation.** Mirror checks and shoulder checks, especially when turning (watch for cyclists!) and changing lanes. Examiners specifically watch whether the head turn is visible.
3. **Speed.** Too fast in 30 km/h zones, but also consistently too slow and hesitant, both get marked down.
4. **Behavior at crosswalks and bus stops.**
5. **Maneuvers.** Clipping the curb while parking, an emergency stop that's too timid.

## What a failed attempt costs

- TÜV/DEKRA exam fee plus the driving school's re-presentation fee: usually **€250 to €350** together
- Extra lessons before the new date: realistically **€300 to €500**
- You can retake it after **two weeks** at the earliest, and open slots often take longer

A failed attempt runs a good €600 and several weeks. [Here's exactly what happens after a fail](/blog/failed-german-driving-test/). So don't book the exam when you *feel* ready, book it when your last few drives were objectively mistake-free. And if nerves are the real issue, [driving test anxiety is very treatable](/blog/driving-test-anxiety-germany/).

## How to prepare in a way you can actually measure

- **Know the test area.** Test routes almost always run through the same right-before-left networks and 30 km/h zones. Walk or drive them beforehand.
- **Track mistakes instead of guessing.** After every lesson, note which mistake categories still show up. You're exam-ready when the critical categories (right of way, observation) sit at zero across several drives in a row.
- **Practice the examiner's phrasing.** "Turn right at the next intersection," "drive where signage allows, then turn around." Knowing the commands means nothing catches you off guard.
- **Drill the maneuvers to the point of boredom.** [Parking](/blog/learn-to-parallel-park-germany/) on command has to work even when you're nervous, and the [shoulder check](/blog/shoulder-check-german-driving-test/) has to be automatic.
- **Exam day basics.** Well rested, arrive 15 minutes early, bring glasses if you need them, bring ID.

## After you pass

Two years of probation, zero-tolerance alcohol limits for new drivers under 21, and the license itself is valid for 15 years initially. And if you're converting a license from abroad: [different rules apply, with no mandatory lessons](/blog/convert-foreign-licence-germany-overview/).
