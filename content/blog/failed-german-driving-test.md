First, the number nobody tells you beforehand: **roughly a third** of practical driving tests in Germany end in a fail. It is one of the strictest driving exams in the world, and failing it says very little about your driving and nothing about your final outcome. Most people pass on the second attempt. Here is the complete playbook for what comes next.

## What happens right after the test

The examiner tells you the result immediately and names the decisive mistakes. Your driving school receives the official protocol. Ask your instructor to walk through it with you: it states in writing exactly why you failed, and it is the raw material for your second attempt.

## When can you retake it?

The earliest retake is **two weeks** after the failed attempt. In practice it usually takes longer because exam slots are scarce, expect **two to six weeks** depending on your region. There is no nationwide limit on attempts, and the often-repeated mandatory break after a third fail no longer exists in that form. Your driving school knows the local rules of your test center.

## What a retake really costs

| Item | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| New TÜV/DEKRA exam fee | ca. €130 |
| Driving school presentation fee (car + instructor) | €100-190 |
| Extra practice lessons before the retake (3-6 lessons) | €250-550 |

A failed attempt realistically costs **€600 or more**. Annoying, but it is also your biggest lever: preparing attempt two properly is much cheaper than rushing into attempt three.

## The four mistakes that fail most candidates

German examiners fail people on observation and right-of-way, almost never on car control:

- **Right of way**, especially the rechts-vor-links rule at unmarked intersections
- **Missing shoulder checks** (Schulterblick) when turning, changing lanes, or pulling out
- **Turning errors**, wrong lane positioning and not checking for cyclists
- **Speed management**, too fast in 30 zones or hesitantly slow elsewhere

Match your protocol mistakes to these categories and you can practice precisely instead of just buying more lessons.

## A note for non-German speakers

The theory exam is available in English and several other languages. The practical exam is conducted in German, but the examiner's instructions are short and standardized ("an der nächsten Kreuzung rechts", "bitte parken Sie rückwärts ein"). Drilling the twenty or so command phrases with your instructor removes most of the language risk. If a language issue contributed to your fail, say so and practice exactly those commands.

## The plan for attempt two

1. **Debrief the protocol** with your instructor and sort each mistake into the categories above.
2. **Practice narrow, not broad.** Two to four lessons that deliberately drive your problem situations beat six generic rounds.
3. **Keep momentum between lessons.** Log mistakes, rehearse procedures, learn your city's typical exam routes. The [DriveDE app](https://www.drivede.app) logs mistakes with one tap during practice drives and shows whether your problem categories are actually shrinking.

5. **Take exam nerves seriously.** If your head failed the test rather than your driving, tell your instructor. A full dress-rehearsal drive under exam conditions works wonders.
