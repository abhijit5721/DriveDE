No single mistake ends more German driving tests than a missed shoulder check. The reason is simple: it covers your **blind spot**, the area just behind and to the side of you that neither your mirror nor your side mirrors show, and that's exactly where cyclists and motorcycles show up. Examiners grade it without mercy, and only if it's **visible**.

## When is a shoulder check (*Schulterblick*) required?

Rule of thumb: **any time you change your line of travel sideways**. Specifically:

| Situation | Check which side |
|---|---|
| Pulling off from the curb | left |
| Changing lanes | the side you're moving toward |
| Overtaking (pulling out and back in) | left, then right |
| Turning right | right (watch for cyclists!) |
| Turning left with a bike lane or multiple lanes | left |
| Parking in or pulling out | the side you're moving toward |
| Exiting a roundabout | right |
| Passing an obstacle | the side you're pulling toward |

The single most important case is **turning right**: the look over your right shoulder before you turn in is the cyclist check, and it's exactly where most people fail.

## How to actually do it

1. **Order of operations.** Interior mirror, side mirror, **then** shoulder check, then signal or steering input. The shoulder check is the last check, not the first.
2. **Which window?** Over your shoulder through the **rear side window**, not the mirror, not the rear windshield. Turn your head roughly 90 degrees, one short, deliberate look into the blind spot.
3. **Short, not long.** One second is enough. Look back too long and you create a new mistake up front.
4. **Visible to the examiner.** The examiner sits behind you and grades what they can see. A clear head turn counts, a glance of the eyes doesn't.

## How it becomes a habit

The shoulder check rarely fails because of a knowledge gap, it fails because it hasn't been automated under stress. What actually works:

- **Say it out loud** during your first lessons: "mirror, shoulder, signal." Feels silly, wires in the sequence anyway.
- **Practice as a passenger.** Mentally drive along on every ride and spot the moments that call for a shoulder check.
- **Count mistakes instead of guessing.** Log after every lesson how often the shoulder check was missing. In the [DriveDE app](https://www.drivede.app), "missed shoulder check" is its own one-tap mistake in the drive log, so you can see over several weeks whether it's actually dropping to zero before you book the exam.

## In the context of the actual exam

A single missed shoulder check in a low-risk situation usually isn't a fail on its own, a pattern of them is, and turning right with cyclists around, a single miss can be enough.
