Japan is on Germany's **Anlage 11 FeV** list, which puts Japanese licence holders in the best possible position: your licence is exchanged for a German one **without any theory or practical exam**. The whole process is administrative.

## The 6-month rule

From the day you register your residence in Germany (Anmeldung), your Japanese licence remains valid for **6 months** of driving. After that you need the German licence in hand. Because appointment lead times plus processing can easily eat 2 to 3 months, apply as soon as you have your registration certificate.

## Step by step

1. **Book an appointment** at the Führerscheinstelle responsible for your district.
2. **Get an official translation** of your Japanese licence. German offices accept translations from **ADAC or a sworn translator**; many Japanese expats use the German translation issued via JAF (Japan Automobile Federation) or the Japanese consulate. Confirm with your specific office which source it accepts.
3. **Prepare the documents**: passport, Anmeldebestätigung, one biometric photo, the original licence and the translation.
4. **Submit the application** and pay the fee, usually €35 to €45.
5. **Exchange the card**: when the German licence is ready, the office usually keeps the Japanese licence or returns it to the issuing authority. If you plan to drive in Japan again, ask about getting it back before surrendering it; practice varies.

## What you do NOT need

- No theory exam, no practical exam
- No driving school and no mandatory lessons
- Usually no eye test and no first-aid course (individual offices occasionally ask for an eye test, about €7)

## Things that trip people up

- **Issue date matters.** The licence must have been issued before you took up residence in Germany. If your card only shows the latest renewal date, bring proof of the original issue date (Japanese licences show this as the top date row; a consulate letter helps in unclear cases).
- **Left-hand to right-hand traffic.** Nothing in the paperwork prepares you for driving on the right. Plan a few calm practice drives before commuting.
- **International Driving Permits are not a substitute.** A 1949-Convention IDP from Japan only covers short visits, not residence.

Processing typically takes 3 to 8 weeks.

## After the exchange

German traffic culture has its own sharp edges: unrestricted Autobahn sections, right-before-left in residential areas, and strict priority rules for trams and cyclists. Even exam-free drivers benefit from a structured refresher before daily driving.
