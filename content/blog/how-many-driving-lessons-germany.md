Short answer: **only 12 lessons are legally required (the Sonderfahrten, or special drives). On average, German learners add 25 to 35 practice lessons on top, so 37 to 47 lessons total.** The law sets no minimum for practice lessons, in practice your instructor decides when you're ready to be presented for the exam.

| | Lessons at 45 min each |
|---|---|
| Mandatory (Sonderfahrten) | 12 |
| Practice lessons, average | 25-35 |
| **Total, average** | **37-47** |
| Cost at €80/practice lesson | roughly €2,500-4,500 total |

## The mandatory lessons (Sonderfahrten)

| Special drive | Number at 45 min |
|---|---|
| Rural road drive (Überlandfahrt) | 5 |
| Autobahn drive | 4 |
| Night drive | 3 |
| **Total** | **12** |

These 12 lessons are fixed by law and identical at every driving school. Everything else is between you and how fast you're learning.

## What the average actually looks like

Across Germany, learners average **25 to 35 practice lessons plus the 12 mandatory ones**, so 37 to 47 total. At €70 to €95 per practice lesson, that's by far the biggest line item in the [total cost of a German license](/blog/german-driving-licence-cost/).

The range is huge: some pass with 20 total lessons, others need 60-plus. The main factors:

- **Age.** Statistically, 17- and 18-year-olds learn faster than someone learning at 35.
- **City vs. rural.** Big-city traffic (Munich, Hamburg, Berlin) takes more lessons than a small town.
- **Frequency.** Two lessons a week beats one lesson a week by a wide margin, because you forget less in between.
- **Review between lessons.** The most underrated lever, see below.
- **Prior experience.** Mopeds, scooters, driving practice abroad.

## The lever nobody talks about: what happens between lessons

A lesson costs €80 and lasts 45 minutes. Half of what you learn in it is gone a week later if you don't reinforce it. That's exactly how the expensive repeat loops happen: your instructor practices the same intersection for the fourth time because nothing was logged or reviewed in between.

What measurably helps:

1. **Log mistakes**, right after each lesson. Not "it went badly," but specific: "missed shoulder check on a lane change, twice."
2. **Rehearse procedures mentally.** Hill starts, parking, roundabouts, walk through the sequence in your head.
3. **Learn the test area**, as a passenger or on foot. Where are the right-before-left networks, where are the 30 km/h zones?
4. **Measure progress instead of guessing.** Which mistakes still show up, which ones have been gone for three lessons straight?

## When are you actually exam-ready?

Not when you feel good about it, but when your last few drives were objectively low on mistakes: no right-of-way errors, consistent mirror and shoulder checks, confident handling of right-before-left. Every exam attempt costs €250 to €350 plus extra lessons, and a third fail the first time, [here's what happens if that's you](/blog/failed-german-driving-test/). The cheapest lesson you'll ever take is the one that prevents a failed attempt. The [DriveDE app](https://www.drivede.app) measures exactly that: a mistake log per drive and an objective readiness score, free.
