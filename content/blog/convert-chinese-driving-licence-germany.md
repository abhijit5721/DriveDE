China is **not** on Germany's Anlage 11 FeV list, so a Chinese licence cannot be exchanged administratively: you must pass the **German theory and practical exams**. The counterweight that most people learn too late: converters have **no mandatory lesson hours**. First-time learners must buy 12 Sonderfahrten; you buy only the lessons you actually need.

## Driving before the conversion

China is not a party to the international driving permit conventions that Germany recognizes, so a Chinese licence alone does not entitle you to drive here on arrival the way many other licences do. Whether and how you may drive during your first months depends on having a **certified German translation** of your licence and on your residence situation. Clarify this with your Führerscheinstelle before driving anything; the general 6-month window still frames the timeline for the conversion itself.

## Step by step

1. **Apply at the Führerscheinstelle** with: passport, Anmeldebestätigung, biometric photo, your Chinese licence and a **certified translation** (ADAC or a sworn translator; some offices accept specific notarized translations from China, most do not, so budget for a German one), an **eye test** (about €7) and a **first-aid certificate** (9 units, €40 to €60).
2. **Register with a driving school** to book both exams through TÜV or DEKRA. State clearly that this is an Umschreibung and ask for an assessment drive.
3. **Theory exam**: 30 official catalogue questions. The exam can be sat in **Mandarin** as well as English and German. Do not underestimate it: the German catalogue is scenario-based and much larger than the Chinese subject-1 question pool people remember.
4. **Practical exam**: about 55 minutes in real traffic with a TÜV/DEKRA examiner, conducted in German. Chinese practical training is heavily course-based (closed practice areas); the German exam is entirely on public roads, which is the single biggest adjustment.

## Where Chinese licence holders typically lose points

- **Right-before-left** at unmarked junctions: no equivalent habit exists, and it is on every exam route.
- **Interaction with cyclists**: mandatory shoulder checks before right turns, generous passing distance, and yielding at bike crossings.
- **Autobahn procedure**: merging at speed and strict keep-right discipline.
- **Maneuvers on command**: reverse parking and turning in the road are requested verbally in German; rehearse the commands.

## Realistic budget

| Item | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Application, translation, eye test, first aid | €120–180 |
| Theory exam fee + materials | €60–110 |
| Driving school admin fee | €100–250 |
| Driving lessons (typical converter: 6–18) | €420–1,700 |
| Practical exam fee | €230–320 |
| Total | **€930–2,550** |

Because Chinese training rarely includes open-road routine, converters from China often need a few more lessons than other groups. That makes structured practice between paid lessons the biggest cost lever of all.

## Plan it like an exam campaign

Two exams, one right-before-left rule, and a road-based test format you have not trained in. None of it is hard with deliberate preparation; all of it is expensive without. Track your readiness objectively and book the practical only when the data says pass.
