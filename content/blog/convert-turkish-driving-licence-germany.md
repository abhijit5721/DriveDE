Turkey is **not** on Germany's Anlage 11 FeV list, so a Turkish licence cannot simply be swapped: you must pass the German **theory exam and practical exam**. The good news that offsets it: converters have **no mandatory lesson hours**. Unlike a first-time learner, you skip the 12 compulsory Sonderfahrten entirely and only pay for the practice you genuinely need.

One more piece of good news specific to Turkish speakers: the official German theory exam can be taken in **Turkish**. You do not need exam-level German to get through the theory.

## The 6-month rule

After registering your residence (Anmeldung), your Turkish licence stays valid in Germany for **6 months**. The conversion, including exams, typically takes 3 to 6 months, so the safe move is to apply in your first weeks.

## Step by step

1. **Apply at the Führerscheinstelle** with: passport or ID, Anmeldebestätigung, a biometric photo, your Turkish licence and a **certified translation** (ADAC or a sworn translator), an **eye test** (about €7) and a **first-aid course certificate** (9 units, €40 to €60).
2. **Join a driving school** to book the exams through TÜV or DEKRA. Say clearly that this is an Umschreibung: you want an assessment drive and exam preparation, not a beginner package.
3. **Theory exam**: 30 questions from the official catalogue, available in Turkish, German, English and other languages. The question style is scenario-based with multiple correct answers per question, which surprises people used to the Turkish test format. Work through the full official catalogue at least once.
4. **Practical exam**: about 55 minutes with a TÜV/DEKRA examiner, conducted in German. Learn the standard commands (rechts abbiegen, wenden, rückwärts einparken) even if your instructor teaches in Turkish.

## What Turkish drivers say surprises them

- **Right-before-left** at unmarked residential junctions is enforced ruthlessly in the exam.
- **Bicycle infrastructure**: shoulder checks before every right turn are mandatory, and examiners watch for them specifically.
- **Procedure over flow**: German examiners score the textbook sequence (mirror, shoulder, signal) higher than smooth-but-casual driving.

## Realistic budget

| Item | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Application, translation, eye test, first aid | €120-180 |
| Theory exam fee + materials | €60-110 |
| Driving school admin fee | €100-250 |
| Driving lessons (typical converter: 5-15) | €350-1,400 |
| Practical exam fee | €230-320 |
| Total | **€850-2,250** |

Experienced drivers who prepare in a structured way often stay near the bottom of that range; the expensive path is drifting through open-ended lessons without measuring progress.

## Keep your driving experience, replace the habits

You already know how to drive. What the German exam tests is whether you drive the German way: priority rules, procedure and calm predictability. Focus your limited paid lessons on exactly the habits that differ, and use every drive in between to rehearse them deliberately.
