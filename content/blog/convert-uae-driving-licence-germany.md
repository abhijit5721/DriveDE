The United Arab Emirates is **not** on Germany's Anlage 11 FeV list. Converting a UAE licence therefore requires passing **both the German theory exam and the practical exam**. The good news that changes the whole calculation: as a converter you have **no mandatory lesson hours**. No 12 Pflichtstunden, no Sonderfahrten. You buy exactly as many lessons as you need, and at €70 to €95 per lesson that freedom is worth thousands.

## The 6-month rule

After registering residence (Anmeldung) you may drive on your UAE licence for **6 months**, usually with an International Driving Permit or a certified translation. After that, no driving until the German licence is issued. The conversion realistically takes 3 to 6 months end to end, so apply in your first weeks, not month five.

## Step by step

1. **Apply at the Führerscheinstelle**: passport, Anmeldebestätigung, biometric photo, your UAE licence plus a **certified translation** (ADAC or a sworn translator), an **eye test** (about €7) and a **first-aid course certificate** (9 units, €40 to €60).
2. **Register with a driving school.** You formally need one to book the exams through TÜV or DEKRA even though no minimum lessons apply. Say upfront that it is an Umschreibung: a good school assesses you in one or two drives instead of selling a beginner package.
3. **Pass the theory exam.** 30 questions from the official catalogue, available in **English** and several other languages. The style is scenario-based with multiple correct answers, noticeably different from the RTA test, so drill the official catalogue rather than relying on experience.
4. **Pass the practical exam.** Around 55 minutes with a TÜV/DEKRA examiner, conducted in German. The examiner commands are short and standardized; learn them even if your instructor teaches in English.

## Where UAE licence holders typically struggle

- **Right before left.** Unmarked residential junctions give priority to the right, with no signage. UAE roads are almost entirely signal-controlled, so this rule has no habit to attach to, and it is the top exam-failure cause for converters.
- **Narrow streets, bikes and trams.** German city driving is dense: parked cars, cyclists with priority, 30 zones. It is the opposite of wide Gulf highways, and examiners watch your cyclist checks on every right turn.
- **Strict procedure.** Mirror, shoulder check, signal, in that order, every single lane change. UAE driving tolerates a more fluid style; German examiners do not.
- **Speed discipline downwards.** The challenge is not driving fast on the Autobahn, it is consistently holding 30 in a 30 zone.
