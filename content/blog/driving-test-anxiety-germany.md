First, the relief: being nervous before the driving test is the norm, not the exception. Examiners know this and factor it in, a shaky start from nerves alone doesn't fail anyone. Anxiety only becomes a problem when it blocks your observation and decision-making. That part you can actually train against.

## Why driving test anxiety is different

Unlike a written exam, someone is grading you live from the back seat, and you have to act instead of think it through. The feeling of "being watched" eats up the mental bandwidth you actually need for watching traffic. So the fix isn't "just relax," it's: automate the driving tasks enough that they still work on half your normal bandwidth.

## What measurably helps

1. **A real mock exam.** Ask your instructor for a full exam simulation: only standardized examiner phrases, no help, no comments, 45 minutes, ideally in the actual test area. The first run feels awful, the second feels normal. That habituation effect is the entire point.
2. **Objective readiness instead of a gut feeling.** A big chunk of the anxiety is just not knowing whether you're actually ready. If your last five drives were documented as low-mistake (no right-of-way errors, shoulder checks solid), that's a fact anxiety can't argue with. The [DriveDE app](https://www.drivede.app) makes exactly this visible: a mistake log per drive and a readiness score. Book the exam when the numbers say so, not when the calendar is pushing you.
3. **Know the commands.** A surprisingly large source of anxiety is worrying you won't understand the examiner. The phrases are standardized and learnable ("turn left at the next intersection," "please turn around where it's allowed"). Knowing them means nothing catches you off guard.
4. **Breathing as an emergency brake.** When your pulse spikes (before pulling off, at a red light): four seconds in, six to eight seconds out, three rounds. The extended exhale is the fastest built-in calming mechanism you have, and it works fine behind the wheel.
5. **An exam-day routine.** Well rested, eat normally, arrive 15 minutes early, bring ID and glasses if you need them. Skip the extra caffeine. A short warm-up drive with your instructor beforehand helps, starting cold into the exam is unnecessarily hard.
6. **Tell your instructor you're anxious.** Good instructors adjust the last few lessons for it: more simulation, less new material, and they'll pick a time of day when you're sharper.

## What to skip

- **Sedatives or alcohol.** No substances for the exam, nothing untested on exam day. If the anxiety stays paralyzing for weeks, shows up in everyday situations, or gets physically intense, that's worth a conversation with a doctor or therapist, not self-medication.
- **Just "getting it over with" unprepared.** Showing up unready to get rid of the dread tends to produce an [expensive failed attempt](/blog/failed-german-driving-test/) and more anxiety before round two.
- **Horror stories the night before.** Other people's stories about strict examiners won't help you now. Put the phone down and sleep.

## If it still goes wrong

Roughly a third fail on the first attempt, and most of those pass on the second. The best news for last: driving test anxiety is almost always a preparation problem wearing a disguise. Once you're objectively exam-ready and have seen it in writing, you get in that car differently.
