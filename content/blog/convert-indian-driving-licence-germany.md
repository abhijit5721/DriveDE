India is **not** on Germany's Anlage 11 FeV list. That means converting an Indian driving licence requires passing **both the theory exam and the practical exam** in Germany. Here is the part most people miss, and it changes the economics completely: as a licence converter you have **no mandatory lesson hours**. No 12 Pflichtstunden, no Sonderfahrten. You take exactly as many driving lessons as you actually need, and at €70 to €95 per lesson that difference is worth thousands of euros.

## The 6-month rule

You may drive on your Indian licence (with an International Driving Permit or a certified translation) for **6 months after registering residence** in Germany. After that, no driving until the German licence is issued. The full conversion realistically takes 3 to 6 months, so start immediately.

## Step by step

1. **Apply at the Führerscheinstelle**: passport, Anmeldebestätigung, biometric photo, your Indian licence plus a **certified translation** (ADAC or a sworn translator), an **eye test** (Sehtest, about €7), and a **first-aid course certificate** (Erste-Hilfe-Kurs, 9 units, €40 to €60).
2. **Register with a driving school.** Formally you need one to book the exams through TÜV or DEKRA, even though no minimum lessons apply. Tell them upfront you are converting (Umschreibung): a good school will assess you in 1 or 2 drives instead of selling you a full beginner package.
3. **Pass the theory exam.** 30 questions from the official catalogue; you can sit it in **English** (and several other languages). Failing costs time and a new fee, so drill the catalogue properly. Expect the German question style to feel different from Indian tests: scenario-based, multiple correct answers.
4. **Pass the practical exam.** Around 55 minutes with a TÜV/DEKRA examiner. The exam is conducted in German, so learn the standard examiner commands even if your instructor speaks English with you.

## Where Indian licence holders typically struggle

- **Right-before-left.** Unmarked residential junctions give priority to the right. There is no equivalent habit to fall back on, and it is the number one exam failure cause for converters.
- **Right-hand traffic.** India drives on the left. Roundabouts, turning across traffic and shoulder checks all mirror-flip. Give yourself real practice time for this even if you have driven for 15 years.
- **Strict procedure.** Examiners expect textbook mirror-shoulder-signal sequences on every lane change, every time.

## Realistic budget

| Item | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Application, translation, eye test, first aid | €120-180 |
| Theory exam fee + learning materials | €60-110 |
| Driving school admin fee | €100-250 |
| Driving lessons (typical converter: 5-15) | €350-1,400 |
| Practical exam fee (TÜV/DEKRA + school) | €230-320 |
| Total | **€850-2,250** |

The single biggest lever is how many paid lessons you need before the school lets you book the exam. Structured self-preparation between lessons (knowing the exam routes, the maneuvers, the examiner commands) is what keeps you at the bottom of that range.

## The exams are the hurdle, not the paperwork

For Anlage 11 countries the paperwork is the whole story. For India the paperwork is the easy part; passing two German exams in a right-hand-traffic system is the real project. Treat it like one: track your practice, fix recurring mistakes and go into the exam when you are measurably ready, not when you feel lucky.
